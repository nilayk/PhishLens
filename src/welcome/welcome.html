PhishLens

Installed. Nothing else to set up.

Open Gmail and read your mail as usual. Each message you open is checked as it loads, and a small badge appears beside the sender with what was found.

✓ Low Risk · 4/100 ! Caution · 31/100 ⚠ Suspicious · 58/100 ⛔ High Risk · 82/100 ? Not checked

Click any badge to see the score broken down finding by finding, each with the evidence it came from. Every point is explained; nothing is a black box.

The dashed Not checked badge is the important one. It means PhishLens could not read part of the message — usually because Gmail changed its page structure — so it produced no score at all rather than a reassuring one it had not earned.

What it does with your mail

  • Reads the message you have open, checks it, and discards it. Nothing is written to storage and no message is ever sent anywhere by default.
  • Never opens links, loads images, downloads attachments, or looks up a domain. Every check is textual.
  • Never blocks, deletes, or replies. It is advisory: it tells you what it noticed and leaves the decision to you.

Two permissions, and why

mail.google.com Read the open message in order to check it. This is the only site it runs on.
storage Remember your settings. No message content is stored.

The optional AI layer

The technical checks — sender domains, links, attachments, wording — always run on this device and need no AI. On top of them, PhishLens can ask a language model what it makes of the message's intent. By default that is Chrome's built-in on-device model, if your browser has one; if it does not, everything else still works and the panel says so.

A model can never outvote the checks. It contributes at most 15 of the 100 points, cannot remove a finding, and scores nothing at all unless a technical check already found something.
